Why it matters

An IEP is a promise to a student. Many educators are asked to keep it with little support.

Writing a strong IEP takes time, training, and confidence. Many of the educators we serve are new to special education. Before we began, we asked them how ready they felt.

2.5 out of 5
Average comfort with AI and IEP writing, from the 13 LCSD1 educators who rated it in our opening survey (September 2026). A 5 means very confident.

Where they felt least ready

Average comfort, 1 to 5. Opening survey, September 2026.
How it works

The educator decides. CoIEP drafts. The educator checks.

Each one-hour session is hands-on. Educators work on a real part of an IEP, using the CoIEP platform and a practice sheet we built for that month.

1

Decide first

The educator sets the plan: the student’s need, the skill to target, and what success looks like.

The educator
2

Draft with AI

CoIEP drafts from those decisions and gives feedback against special education law and research.

CoIEP
3

Check and revise

The educator judges the draft, keeps what fits, fixes what does not, and saves both versions.

The educator
Privacy comes first. Educators use anonymous names only. Our practice pages keep work on the educator’s own device until they choose to submit it.

The year ahead

One IEP, built part by part, across the school year.

Each educator saves their draft before and after revising it, so growth in IEP quality can be measured, not just felt.

Present levels and goals

Progress and instruction
