Step 1: Link the PLAAFP to a Standard

Start from your student’s PLAAFP. The standard you choose should be the one most essential to accelerating the student's progress in the general education curriculum, tied to a high-priority need.

The standard

Choose the Wyoming Department of Education content and performance standard that most closely aligns with the identified student need: edu.wyoming.gov/transparency/content-performance-standards

Step 2: Gap Analysis and Skill Components

A gap analysis measures the distance between the student's present level and the grade-level standard, and names the prerequisite skills that close it. Answer the question below, then rate each skill and justify your rating.
See the worked example (Richie, grade 4 mathematics)
PLAAFP (excerpt). Richie can add, subtract, and multiply single-digit numbers with 90% accuracy. He can add and multiply double-digit numbers with 50% accuracy, but he is unable to subtract double-digit numbers that require regrouping. He has not yet learned the process of division.
Standard. Grade 4, use place value understanding and properties of operations to perform multi-digit arithmetic: (5) multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number, and multiply two two-digit numbers, using strategies based on place value and the properties of operations; (6) find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.
Gaps. Multi-digit subtraction with regrouping (not yet); multi-digit multiplication (50% on two-digit); division (not started). Skills needed. Regrouping across place values; multi-digit by one-digit multiplication with place-value strategies; division as the inverse of multiplication. Where the prerequisites are taught. Subtraction with regrouping and the concept of division are grade 3 standards; multi-digit multiplication begins in grade 4. Component to target first. Subtraction with regrouping, because it is the earliest missing prerequisite and the multiplication and division strategies depend on place-value fluency.

Step 3: Set the Goal Components, Then Draft

Criteria before drafting. Decide each part of the goal yourself before you open the Goal Writer. The draft is then checked against your decisions, not against your impression of it. A measurable annual goal names the student and states a behavior or skill, the condition, the criteria, and the timeframe. You decide each of these below, with the criteria in three parts: the mastery criterion, the retention criterion, and the measure. The student appears under the anonymous name you entered above. The goal does not restate the standard, and it does not say what the teacher will do.
Notice. Three of the six are marked Unsure. That is honest, and it tells you where to look first when the draft comes back.

Reference

Steps to developing a standards-based IEP
StepKey elements to consider
1. Use the PLAAFP to link to a standard.What is the student's current performance in relation to the grade-level content standards? What standards has the student achieved? What skills does the student already have that will enable them to work toward grade-level standards? What skills are missing?
2. Choose a standard related to a prioritized need and analyze its skill components.Choose the standard most essential to accelerating progress in the general education curriculum, to educational benefit, and to reducing the difference between the student's performance and grade-level standards (gap analysis).
3. Select a skill component and develop a goal.The goal may address a missing prerequisite skill. It describes what the student will accomplish in one school year. Required components: behavior or skill, conditions, criteria, timeframe.
4. If needed, write objectives or benchmarks.Short-term objectives and benchmarks are steps that measure progress toward the annual goal. They are required for students taking the alternate assessment aligned to alternate achievement standards, and then for every goal on that IEP.
5. Collect progress-monitoring data.Identify the skill in the goal that will be monitored and the measure. Draw an aimline from the baseline to the criterion. Chart results and compare to the aimline. Make instructional adjustments based on the data.
ReminderNot every goal needs to be linked to a state standard, and the team does not write a goal for every standard. Prioritize the standards most likely to maximize progress in the general curriculum. Some students will need functional goals.
Questions to ask about PLAAFPs and standards
  • What has been the student's response to learning strategies? How do learner characteristics affect learning? What interventions or accommodations have worked?
  • Which need presents the biggest obstacle to progress toward grade-level standards?
  • How does the disability affect involvement and progress in the general curriculum? What skill sets does the student need to access it?
  • What is the student's current performance in relation to grade-level standards? Which standards has the student achieved?
  • What skills does the student already have that will enable them to work toward current grade-level standards? What skills are missing?
  • What are this student's high-priority needs that goals might address?
Questions for IEP teams conducting a gap analysis
  • What is the intent of the content standard? What must the student know and be able to do? What are its essential skills?
  • What depth of knowledge? What new skills and extensions?
  • What knowledge and skills should already be in place (prerequisites, connections to previous learning)?
  • What are possible ways for the student to show what they know within the standard?
  • Has the student been taught content aligned with the grade-level standards, with appropriate scaffolding, accessible materials, and any assistive technology needed?
  • Does the student have an appropriate means to express what they know relative to the standard?
Adapted from a Kansas standards-based IEP professional learning handout; the Richie example is from that handout. Alternate-assessment language follows IDEA (34 CFR 300.320(a)(2)(ii)).
