1 · Disclaimer or Disclosure?

The Standard asks for plain language, not legal language (Principle 7). These are not the same thing:

A disclaimer protects the writer

Fine print. Added to satisfy a requirement. Nobody is expected to read it.

“Portions of this document may have been generated with the assistance of artificial intelligence technologies.”

A disclosure informs the reader

Communication. Says what happened, who decided, and welcomes questions.

“We drafted parts of this with a GenAI tool, reviewed and individualized everything ourselves, and we’re happy to talk about how.”

4 · The Log Behind the Disclosure

A disclosure says “every element was reviewed and individualized.” Your Instructional Decision Log is what makes that sentence true - it is the record of the reviewing and individualizing.

With a GenAI tool

The log records your prompt, what the tool returned, what you accepted, modified, or rejected, and why. It is the evidence that AI drafted and you decided.

Without a GenAI tool

Drafts from templates, colleagues, or your own writing carry the same risks: generic content, unverified sources, criteria you never wrote down. The log asks the same questions of every draft, whatever its origin.

Why it matters: it turns your reasoning into a record you can stand on with families, teams, and reviewers; it shows your own growth as Guessed entries become Confident; and it enacts the non-negotiable - no edit without a record. Declining to use AI is a legitimate professional position; the log documents that reasoning with exactly the same respect.
The Standard’s guiding principle: when in doubt, protect the student. Applied to this page: when in doubt, disclose.
